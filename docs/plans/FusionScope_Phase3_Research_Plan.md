# Phase III experiment protocol

**Question.** Given a two-layer program that must return its usual tensor and two exact positive-activation counts on every invocation, can a selector choose a better execution schedule than always using joint reduction? How many future invocations pay for making that choice?

The CPU protocol was registered before implementation. All earlier outcomes, including the previous confirmation cohorts and two ResNet blocks, were available during development. Section 4 specifies the fresh test workloads. The GPU arm remains prospective. [Original registration](https://github.com/ZZzJunzum/Compiler_report/blob/96bf2aedfc8041bcd63826e6946a7aedad4b8b84/docs/plans/FusionScope_Phase3_Research_Plan.md) · [Previous CPU evidence](https://github.com/ZZzJunzum/Compiler_report/tree/e5964158e339cb1ff866b5f1ca4d0ba3bc9b6325) · [Completed CPU study](../../results/phase3/cpu/analysis/endpoint.json)

## 1. Motivation and predictions

| Observation from Phases I–II | Testable prediction | What would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| All four placements collapsed in 32 synthetic graphs. Twenty-four one-tile schedules matched the separate-count HLO in Phase II. | Some syntactically different schedules have identical optimized modules. Predicting a large cost difference for them is a detectable model error. | New shape, backend or ReLU controls produce distinct fingerprints, or equivalent modules repeatedly differ beyond independent repeatability bounds. Retain these outcomes separately. |
| On the million-element affine branch, joint counting eliminated two 4 MiB exported masks. A 16-row tile also removed the full masks while retaining seven tagged duplicate arithmetic sites. | Changing the aggregation order and changing the tile extent can affect different costs. The saving from a joint sum may change when the two masks already fit in a small tile. | For each tested tile, the joint-versus-separate timing contrast and its interaction with tile size remain below the measured floor, even where HLO structure changes. |
| In all 186 frozen comparisons the five analytic features make the joint schedule componentwise cheaper or equal. On the 256-by-256 normalization branch the observed 16-row tile took 184.15 µs versus 360.10 µs for joint counting. | Allowing per-tile kernel throughput and the distinction between live capacity and repeated accesses will let a model predict some real ranking reversals. | The extended model becomes capable of predicting reversals but fails to identify them on the fresh shapes, or matched-cost fixed policies perform equally well. |
| Measuring all legal separate-count tiles in discovery selected a schedule within the A/A floor on 26/32 conditions. | A two-candidate policy can retain part of this benefit at a lower setup cost. | A rule that simply times joint reduction and a fixed legal tile does as well, or the setup cost exceeds savings over the declared invocation horizon. |

## 2. Work packages and freeze order

1. **Reconstruct the published controls (day 1).** Recompute the 186 dominance comparisons, ridge's 12 departures from joint counting and the 24 HLO-equivalence controls. Verify the old measurement and prediction hashes. Keep the feature values and expression used in each comparison. [Frozen inputs](../../results/phase2/model/features.csv) · [Held-out decisions](../../results/phase2/model/heldout_results.csv)
2. **Run the mechanism experiment (days 2–3).** Implement a tiled joint reduction and tiled unobserved controls. Execute the crossed experiment in Section 3, audit five output paths or loop-boundary values per representative case, and save every size and failure.
3. **Develop and calibrate on old cases (days 4–5).** Measure the new tiled-joint candidates alongside their separate counterparts and V0/V1/V2/V3 on the 32 old conditions. Include the whole-extent tile even if it was missing from the old grid. Fit/calibrate the models in Section 5. Use whole-family outer folds and training-only inner selection. Re-run the legacy controls in the same process blocks as the new candidates to expose run-to-run shifts.
4. **Lock before collecting new outcomes (day 6).** Commit the generator, the 28-case manifest, seeds, exact candidate lists and ordering, model parameters, thresholds, fold membership, machine fingerprint, all static choices and both two-candidate shortlists. A digest covers these files. No new-case candidate timing or optimized candidate IR enters the static arm before the lock.
5. **Run the locked evaluation (days 7–8).** Perform budget-limited discovery, lock its choices, then collect independent exhaustive confirmation. Evaluate every eligible schedule regardless of the recommendation. The four mixed cases are reported separately.
6. **Analyze and publish (days 9–10).** Produce the mechanism contrasts, equivalence controls, held-out decisions, uncertainty, setup costs and break-even counts. Re-run the mechanism cases from a clean checkout. If a GPU is available, run Section 7 as a separate device study. These workday estimates do not condition acceptance on obtaining a GPU.

## 3. CPU mechanism experiment

Use E3 branch, N2 branch, M3 chain and C3 chain. Within each graph cross three domain sizes with three observer settings. At whole extent use the direct untiled implementation. The smaller extents are rows for E/M/N and whole images for C.

| Graph | Extents per invocation of the body | Observer settings at every extent |
|---|---|---|
| E3 branch, 4,096 × 256 | Whole, 16, 64 rows | Main output only, two independent sums, one variadic joint sum |
| N2 branch, 256 × 256 | Whole, 4, 16 rows | Same three |
| M3 chain, 256 × 256 | Whole, 16, 64 rows | Same three |
| C3 chain, 16 × 32 × 32 × 16 | Whole, 2, 8 samples | Same three |

This yields **36 graph/schedule configurations**. Keep loop-carried output construction identical between the two observed arms. Match the main-output tiling in the unobserved arm. The count semantics, input, weights, dtype, device placement and integer accumulator are identical within a graph. Preserve the complete reduction domain of a normalization row and the complete spatial halo of each convolution sample.

For process medians at extent (b), compute (O_s(b)=t_s(b)-t_0(b)), where (s) is separate or joint. Compute the paired interaction (D(b)=[t_{sep}(b)-t_{joint}(b)]-[t_{sep}(whole)-t_{joint}(whole)]). Use four aliases of the same executable to form signed four-label pseudo-interactions under the same randomized design. Compare the median interaction with the 95th percentile of their absolute values and report the process-level signs. A tile can speed up the unobserved main path too. Subtracting its matched unobserved control prevents assigning that entire gain to the observer.

For each program trace the main output, two counts, producer at the first activation, and mask or partial-sum boundary. Record source and HLO lines, marked duplicates, full mask bytes, maximum tile-local live bytes, logical elements visited times loop trips, reduction depth, loop count, temporary allocation and both stage calls. Compare normalized HLO to identify one-tile controls. Record CPU cycles/instructions and available cache or memory-controller events in **separate profiling runs** where permissions allow. Preserve event names, scope and multiplexing ratios. Without counters, report structural evidence and timings, leaving physical traffic unmeasured.

## 4. Fresh workloads and exact candidate counts

The first 24 conditions are the primary test. For each family and size, run both the original chain and branch. Within each condition use one fixed weight set seeded with 1103 and the input seeds 101, 4471 and 8231. Time seed 101. Keep all comparisons within the same device. Count scalars must match exact host counts from the diagnostic preactivations, and the main output must meet the published per-family FP32 tolerances. Use the existing normalization tolerance for mixed graphs. Preserve failure inputs, the chosen policy and the failed configuration. Report invalid choices as failures and never use test results to repair a choice.

| Test group | Shapes | Conditions | Configurations including unobserved controls |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Elementwise, dense, row normalization | Each family uses (rows, width) = (32,192), (128,320), (512,640) | 18 | 252 |
| Residual convolution, NHWC | (2,24,24,24), (4,40,40,32), (8,48,48,48) | 6 | 60 |
| Unseen compositions | Row normalization → dense and dense → row normalization, each with (32,320) and (512,192), chain only | 4 | 56 |
| **Total** | **Primary and transfer sets remain separately identifiable** | **28** | **368** |

Both mixed templates have a ReLU and count after each of the two stages, identical widths at the two count sites, and the original chain readout. They test operator composition. They are synthetic transfer workloads. Replays of the known Flax R1/R2 can provide compatibility checks separately.

Each case includes V0 (main only), V1 (independent), V2 (joint), V3 (two executable stages), plus **two schedules per legal tile**. T-sep computes two independent partial sums per tile, and T-joint computes them with one variadic reduction. Their final scalar merges and concatenation of the main result are identical. Candidate sizes are the unique divisors of the first dimension (B) in ({1,4,16,64,256,B}) for row workloads, or ({1,2,4,8,16,B}) for convolution. Include full-extent controls even if the compiler later erases them. Thus there are **312 configurations in the primary set**, **56 in transfer**, and **340 exact-observation candidates** across both sets. V0 is excluded from the feasible observation oracle.

## 5. Models, cost of information and fair baselines

| Policy | Inputs available when choosing | What is charged |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed joint, fixed tile | Shape and legality. Fixed tile is T-sep at the largest legal extent ≤16. | Compile and run the chosen schedule. |
| Published analytic and ridge | Baseline HLO and declared schedule. Replay them unchanged on their original V1–V4 domain, where their schedule encodings are defined. | Baseline extraction plus chosen compilation. Report domain coverage and regret against both the common-domain and expanded oracles. |
| Revised static analytic model | Baseline HLO, work per tile, total visits, peak live bytes, number of tiles, operator and device-specific calibrated rates. | Baseline compilation or extraction, calibration policy as below, and chosen compilation. |
| Static pairwise ranker | Same deployable inputs. Learn the sign of the within-case latency difference and explicit interactions of operator type, tile size and working-set fraction. | Same static budget. Within each graph, train primarily on pairs separated by the development A/A floor. Predeclare a deterministic tie order headed by V2. |
| Two timed candidates | Always include V2 and the best distinct alternative from the frozen static ranker. The budget-matched control instead tries V2 and the fixed tile. | Compilation, warm-ups and timed trials for **both**, including rejected candidates. Use discovery A/A; revert to V2 if a gain is unresolved. |
| Candidate-IR diagnostic and exhaustive search | Compiled candidate IR can reveal actual mask exports, loop bodies and duplicate fingerprints. Exhaustive confirmation supplies the within-case oracle. | Charge every required candidate compilation. Report this as a separate information budget. The oracle is a reference bound. |

For the analytic extension, predict live capacity, cumulative element visits and bytes crossing a stage boundary as separate quantities. Estimate arithmetic time for each tiled dot/conv shape from development-only single-operator microbenchmarks. Derive the cache-capacity knee and stream bandwidth from independent device calibration. The estimate combines a per-tile compute/transfer maximum, count-merge overhead, loop overhead, and host dispatch. Include the whole-extent main body as a control for all tile predictors. Train the pairwise ranker first without interactions, then add schedule × operator and schedule × calibrated-footprint features. Fit only development data. Allow tied predictions for candidate classes proven equivalent within a budget. Static policies cannot consult unseen candidate fingerprints.

On the old outer folds, compare the original analytic formulation, the same formula with volume and live capacity separated, and that model with shape-dependent throughput added. For the learned arm cross two losses (log-latency regression or within-graph pairwise ranking) with two feature sets (main effects or the same features plus operator/tile interactions). Fix the regularization grid and use inner training folds. These four cells isolate the change of loss from the change of representation. Record the number of conditions where joint reduction still componentwise dominates all schedules, as well as prediction accuracy. Choose one static policy by the lowest mean held-family log regret; break ties in favor of the simpler model. Lock it and the ranker's two-candidate shortlist before new measurements. For new candidates, compare revised models to both fixed policies on the identical expanded search space.

## 6. Timing, decision criteria and deliverables

- **Primary endpoint.** Host dispatch to completion of all device-resident outputs, with no host copy. For the 36-case mechanism grid and final confirmation use five independent sequential processes, six warm-ups and 30 randomized interleaved rounds per case per process. Store every sample and process median. Collect separate four-label A/A blocks for V0, V1 and V2. For two-candidate discovery use two fresh processes, six warm-ups and eight interleaved timed rounds, plus V2 A/A. Lock the chosen schedule before exhaustive confirmation begins.
- **Generalization.** Group all schedules and tiles from the same graph together. The primary comparison gives each of the 24 new cases equal weight. Report the four mixed transfers separately. Old R1/R2, clean replays and microbenchmarks never count as fresh test cases. Report any numerical failures for each policy and use the valid oracle only to define a reference time.
- **Primary comparison and margins.** Compare the locked static policy with fixed V2 on the 24 primary cases. Prespecify a useful gain as a geometric-mean selected/V2 latency ratio ≤0.95, a paired 95% bootstrap upper bound below 1.00 and P90 regret no more than two percentage points above fixed V2. Resample sizes within each family as blocks, retaining both topologies together, and resample process indices jointly for all schedules within a condition (10,000 replicates, seed 32041). Report raw family results and leave-one-size-out sensitivity because there are only three size blocks per family. This is the single primary comparison; two-candidate and transfer results are secondary.
- **Decision quality.** Report median/P90/worst regret, absolute selected–best gaps, A/A-equivalent choices, changes from fixed V2 that helped or harmed, and rank accuracy on resolvable pairs. Keep absolute-time and log-ratio errors as diagnostics. For any apparent win on matching HLO, inspect emitted code and repeatability before attributing an implementation change.
- **Setup cost.** For policy $p$ and reuse count $H$, report $C_p+H T_p$, with $C_p$ covering feature extraction, calibration, compilation and exploratory invocations. Publish cold-start and reused-calibration costs and the common baseline portion. For a positive per-call saving over fixed joint, the first non-worse integer horizon is $\max(0,\lceil(C_p-C_{V2})/(T_{V2}-T_p)\rceil)$. Otherwise report the sign of the slope and any finite crossover directly. Evaluate $H=1,10,100,1000$. Compare the two-candidate policy with its equally funded control and with fixed V2.
- **Outputs.** Store the preregistered manifest and lock, per-process raw records, checksums, candidate HLO/StableHLO and allocation references, measured-versus-predicted rankings, full timing and budget tables under a new device-specific results directory. Publish a mechanism figure for the crossed schedules, a held-out regret plot with A/A equivalence, and a setup-versus-reuse plot. If no policy meets the primary criterion, report the negative result and the conditions in which measurement pays.

## 7. GPU validation and server request

An NVIDIA GPU is needed to test the XLA:GPU execution path, launch count and device-memory traffic. It is not required to establish the CPU dominance result or run the fresh CPU evaluation. Access to **one Linux NVIDIA CUDA GPU available exclusively during measurements** is sufficient for this single-device arm. Keep the device model, driver, JAX/jaxlib/CUDA plugin, XLA flags, clocks/power state, visible device, memory size and L2 size with the data. Use a supported JAX GPU build and confirm where arrays and executables run. A session that permits Nsight Systems traces is enough for launch attribution. Nsight Compute performance-counter access is additionally needed for hardware byte counts. [JAX installation](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/installation.html) · [NVIDIA's JAX profiling guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/JAX-Toolbox/blob/main/docs/profiling.md)

1. **Cross-backend replay.** Begin with the 36 configurations of Section 3. Add R1/R2 with V0–V3 (8 more). Keep FP32, whole summation domains, forward outputs and validation identical. Record actual matmul precision/TF32 settings. Use the device's own unobserved numerical reference and exact count checks, and log cross-device agreement separately. Inference timings use independent unprofiled runs and GPU-specific A/A.
2. **Launches.** Capture optimized GPU HLO, buffer assignment where available, and a short post-warm-up Nsight Systems CUDA timeline. Mark one observer invocation and synchronize its complete outputs. For each marked range report kernel names/counts, launches attributable to the main computation and count reductions where identifiable, and the union of GPU-busy intervals when streams overlap. An HLO fusion count alone does not identify the launch count. [OpenXLA GPU lowering](https://openxla.org/xla/hlo_to_thunks) · [Nsight Systems guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-systems/UserGuide/index.html)
3. **Physical traffic.** On E3 and the normalization branch, profile V1, V2, T-sep(16) and T-joint(16) from separate post-warm-up runs. Query available hardware counters, then record DRAM bytes read/written and L2 traffic with units and kernel coverage. Aggregate over the complete invocation, including final merges. Capture fixed-input warm reuse and a rotating input pool exceeding the reported L2 capacity as separate controls. Sweep E rows to put the *logical size of the two masks* below, near, and above L2 (targets 0.5×, 1× and 4×, rounded to 256-row multiples, subject to available device memory). Report any omitted size. Profiling replay timing is excluded from latency comparisons. [Nsight Compute profiling guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-compute/ProfilingGuide/index.html)
4. **Interpretation.** A joint schedule with fewer HLO exports but unchanged DRAM bytes is a valid result, as are extra launches or a rank reversal. Missing counter permission leaves the traffic question open while the trace can still test launches. No cross-device speedup or GPU selector-generalization claim follows from the CPU coefficients. Validate GPU model selection on a separate locked cohort if that becomes a research objective.
